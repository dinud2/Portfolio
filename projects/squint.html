Squint

QNX (RTOS) · OpenCV · Raspberry Pi 5

Squint is a Google Home-style device that helps elderly couples keep track of their prescriptions and watch for signs of cognitive decline.

View on Devpost

What it is:

Squint is a device designed for elderly couples that sits next to their pill bottles.

  • Recognizes you: When you walk up, it greets you by name through a Google Home and tells you what you take today.
  • Checks the bottle: When you pick one up, it reads the label and checks that it's yours, that it's due, and that you haven't already taken it today.
  • Logs the dose: When your hand goes to your mouth, it records the dose. If it's unsure, it asks you to confirm on screen.
  • Stops double doses: If you reach for a high-risk medication you already took, its eyes turn red and it says, "Stop, Dinu. You already took your Tylenol at 8:02." It also warns you if you grab your partner's bottle.

How it works:

Hardware: Raspberry Pi 5 running QNX 8 (RTOS) and a Logitech webcam.

  1. Camera: QNX didn't have the camera headers we needed, so I wrote our own camera streamer in C using QNX's native camera API. It runs at the highest real-time priority, so the camera keeps streaming no matter how busy the vision models are.
  2. Vision: A Python pipeline uses the one camera feed to answer three questions. Who is it? YuNet finds faces and SFace matches them to each person. Which bottle? A MobileNetV3 network that my teammates and I trained on over 800 photos of medication bottles identifies it. Did they take it? A MediaPipe palm detector watches the area around the mouth.
  3. Running the models: We trained the bottle model locally on my laptop. Every model is then exported to ONNX and run through OpenCV on the Pi.
  4. Face and voice: The Tab5 runs our own Arduino firmware, and its eye colour shows status: white means all good, amber means double-check, red means stop. Speech is generated offline with Piper and played on the Google Home.
  5. App: A SwiftUI iOS app with a widget, Siri shortcuts and notifications.

What I learned:

  • Machine learning models: This project was my first time training and running models. It was a valuable experience that taught me how neural networks work and how computers can learn from different types of data.
  • Raspberry Pi 5: This project taught me how to work with the Raspberry Pi 5 hardware at a low level.

What's next:

  • Design a custom PCB to make Squint smaller.
  • Improve the medication tracking model to support pills in blister packs.
